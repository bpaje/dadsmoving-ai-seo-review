Moving to Manila, Philippines: A Relocation Guide for Expats & Organisations

What to know before shipping a household to Manila—documents, customs treatment, neighbourhood choices, building delivery rules, and organisational coordination.

Quick answer

Shipping a household to Manila may take 4–8 weeks door-to-door illustrative from many origins. Clearance commonly needs an appropriate visa or work basis, a detailed inventory, travel evidence, and proof the shipment is personal rather than commercial; confirm the current case with the Philippine Bureau of Customs.

Philippine Bureau of Customs requirements

Common documents include a valid passport, applicable visa or work authorisation, a complete itemised inventory, and travel evidence connecting the shipment to the relocation. Firearms, regulated electronics, commercial quantities, alcohol, tobacco, and other restricted goods may require separate permits or treatment.

Visa-linked duty treatment

Used household goods may qualify for relief when current eligibility, timing, ownership, and personal-use rules are met. New goods and commercial-scale shipments are more likely to trigger assessment. Dad’s should confirm the client’s exact customs track before packing.

Frequently asked questions

What documents are commonly needed?

A passport, appropriate visa or work authorisation, travel evidence, detailed inventory, and route-specific supporting documents are common. Confirm current Bureau of Customs requirements for the individual case.

Where do relocating families often live?

BGC, Makati, Alabang, and Ortigas are common choices, but school, commute, housing type, and building move-in rules should drive the decision.
