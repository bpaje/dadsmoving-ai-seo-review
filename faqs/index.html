Moving & Storage FAQs

Straight answers on international, office, storage, and large household moves—kept short enough to use during planning and detailed enough to verify.

What changed and whyThis hub faithfully ports the four prototype categories and aligns office answers with the new cluster so the hub and service pages do not contradict one another.

International moves

How far in advance should I book an international move?

Aim for 6–8 weeks before the target ship date for survey, customs documents, and route-specific preparation.

Can I ship a partial container?

Yes. LCL shipping uses shared container space and is often economical for smaller households.

Do you handle customs paperwork?

Dad’s coordinates Philippine export formalities and destination import work with its partner network; the client supplies accurate documents and declarations.

Office moves

Can an office move happen over a weekend?

Yes, subject to building permits, lift windows, security, HVAC, and the written after-hours scope.

Do you label and reinstall workstations?

The scope can include destination tags, cable photos, protected transport, placement, and authorised reconnect tests.

How early should planning begin?

Six to eight weeks is practical for a medium office, and strict buildings or complex IT may need more.

Storage solutions

Is storage climate-controlled and insured?

Options and coverage depend on the item and declared value. Confirm climate requirements, insurance, and exclusions in writing.

Can part of a shipment go to storage?

Yes. Keep direct-delivery and stored inventory separately labelled and release-controlled.

Big household moves

How do you handle a whole house?

Survey it room by room, assign a dedicated lead, sequence packing, and keep essential areas usable as long as practical.

Can you help clear items not moving?

Dad’s can coordinate approved donation, disposal, or storage separately from the delivery shipment.

Start with a free pre-move survey. Dad's will verify access, volume, protection, staffing, and timing before issuing a written estimate.
