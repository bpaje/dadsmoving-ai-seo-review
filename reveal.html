03 — Content SEO, built for how people hire a mover

People don't shop for movers like they shop for shoes.

They're anxious about breakage, cost, and getting stood up on move day. Every piece of new content is built around the three questions that actually decide whether someone books: can I trust you, what will it cost, and do you know my building/route specifically? 04 — Technical SEO, in plain terms

What we're fixing under the hood — and why AI tools care too.

This is the unglamorous plumbing work. None of it is visible to a visitor, but it decides whether Google — and now ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, and Perplexity — can read your site correctly at all.

Fix

Zero meta descriptions, site-wide

We crawled all 31 indexed pages. Not one has a meta description written. This is the single highest-leverage fix on the list — it's the line of text that convinces someone to click your result over a competitor's.

Add

"Labels" that let AI read your site correctly

Structured data (called schema) is invisible extra tagging that spells out "this is a moving company," "this is a price range," "this is a question and its answer" — in a format search engines and AI models can parse with certainty instead of guessing.

Fix

One missing headline, one heading out of order

The homepage has no H1 (every other page does). Separately, on International Moves, a sub-heading ("Door-to-Door Moving Service") currently renders above the section heading it belongs under — small, but it confuses how search engines and screen readers parse the page.

Add

Pages written to answer, not just persuade

Service pages currently lead with brand narrative. We're restructuring the opening of each one to answer the visitor's actual question — cost, timeline, coverage — in the first two sentences, before anything else.

Why this matters beyond Google

When someone types "who should I use to move from Manila to Australia" into ChatGPT instead of Google, the AI doesn't visit ten websites and compare them — it reads whichever pages are the clearest, most specifically-labeled sources on that topic, and answers directly, often naming one company.

That's the whole bet here: the technical fixes and the schema labeling aren't just cleanup — they're what make Dad's the page an AI model can confidently read, trust, and recommend by name instead of a competitor, or nobody at all.
