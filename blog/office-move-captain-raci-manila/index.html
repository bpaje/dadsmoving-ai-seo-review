The controls below are intended for a surveyed, building-approved move plan; project owners should confirm any route, permit, technical, or insurance requirement before execution.

Give one person operational authority

The move captain owns the integrated plan and daily decisions within an agreed threshold. The sponsor resolves business trade-offs; facilities owns sites; IT owns technical change; the mover owns execution safety and custody.

Build a RACI around decisions

List permits, floor-plan freeze, disposal, labels, backups, shutdown, access cards, communications, invoice approval, damage review, and go-live acceptance. Each row needs one accountable owner, even when several people are consulted.

Use standups to remove blockers

In the final two weeks, a 15-minute standup covers changed risks, permits, inventory variance, destination readiness, IT dependencies, user communication, and decisions due that day. A status recital without decisions wastes the meeting.

Run a visible issue log

Record owner, due date, impact, decision needed, and escalation time. During move weekend, split safety-critical, go-live-blocking, and cosmetic issues so the right people respond.

Frequently asked questions

Can the mover be the move captain?

The mover can lead relocation execution, but the client still needs an accountable owner for business priorities, access, IT authority, and acceptance.

How many department coordinators are needed?

Use one per department or practical floor zone—enough to verify ownership and placement without creating a second management hierarchy.
